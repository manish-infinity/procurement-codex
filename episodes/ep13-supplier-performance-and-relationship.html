Episode 13 · Where the transaction becomes a relationship

Supplier Performance & Relationship

Twelve steps have produced a complete transactional record of how a supplier actually behaved. Supplier performance management is what turns that exhaust into a conversation — and it is the first step where the objective is not to process something faster but to make a relationship worth more.

Below: the plain concept → how every major platform handles it → best practice → process mining, AI, orchestration and ownership, stacked until standalone is never enough.

L0 · The Concept

What it is

Supplier performance management measures how suppliers actually deliver against what was agreed. Supplier relationship management decides which suppliers deserve investment beyond measurement. Together they cover segmentation, scorecards, business reviews, corrective action and joint improvement — a governance rhythm rather than a document.

Why it exists

Because the value negotiated in Ep05 and contracted in Ep06 is realised or lost over years of delivery that nobody was watching. Without measurement, poor performance is discovered as a crisis, good performance is never rewarded with more business, and the small number of suppliers who could genuinely change your cost base are treated exactly like everyone else.

What good looks like

Segmentation is honest and small — most suppliers are managed by exception, a handful get real attention. Scorecards are mostly built from transactional data the process already produced rather than from surveys. Reviews happen on a schedule with an agenda and actions that get tracked. Poor performers see a corrective plan with dates, and strategic suppliers see a joint agenda with shared value.

L2 · Best Practice

Design principles
  • Segment before you measure — managing every supplier equally means managing none of them well. Most of the base should be exception-managed, with real governance reserved for the few who carry strategic or risk weight.
  • Score from data you already have — on-time delivery, reject rates, change orders and invoice exceptions are already in the system. A scorecard built mostly from surveys measures how recently someone was annoyed.
  • Measure your own behaviour too — on-time payment, forecast accuracy and specification churn belong on the scorecard. Suppliers perform worse for difficult customers, and refusing to see that makes the whole exercise adversarial.
  • Tie the scorecard to the contract — performance criteria should map to the service levels and obligations agreed in Ep06, or the review becomes an argument about whether the measure was ever agreed.
  • Close the loop or do not open it — a review that produces actions nobody tracks teaches suppliers that the process is ceremonial, and they will resource it accordingly.
  • Reserve relationship investment for where it pays — joint innovation, shared roadmaps and executive sponsorship are expensive. They belong with the small set of suppliers who can move your cost base or your capability, not with whoever shouts loudest.
KPIs & failure modes
  • KPIs — on-time in-full delivery, quality reject rate, responsiveness on change orders, invoice exception rate by supplier, scorecard coverage of spend, action closure rate, savings and value delivered from strategic supplier programmes.
  • Survey theatre — an annual questionnaire circulated to stakeholders, scored subjectively, filed, and never acted on. It consumes goodwill and produces nothing.
  • Everyone is strategic — a segmentation exercise that classifies two hundred suppliers as strategic, guaranteeing that none of them receive strategic attention.
  • Scorecards nobody believes — metrics the supplier disputes because the underlying data is dirty, so every review is spent arguing about the numbers instead of the performance.
  • One-way accountability — measuring the supplier while ignoring late payment, late forecasts and constant specification changes from your own side.
  • Actions without owners — improvement plans with no named owner or date on either side, which close by being forgotten rather than resolved.
  • Performance data that never reaches sourcing — three years of delivery failures that nobody consults when the category is re-tendered in Ep03.

Cheat Sheet — Supplier Performance & Relationship

The 5-second definition

Decide which suppliers matter, measure how they actually deliver using the data the process already produced, meet on a rhythm, and track what you both agreed to change.

KPIs that matter

On-time in-full · quality reject rate · invoice exception rate by supplier · scorecard coverage of spend · action closure rate · value delivered by strategic suppliers.

Scorecard in one line

If your supplier scorecard is mostly opinion, you are measuring your stakeholders mood, not your supplier performance.

Platforms in one line

S2P suites win when performance data comes free from their own transactions; specialists win on supplier data integrity and collaboration depth; nobody wins without governance discipline.

The layers

L0 annual survey → L1 SRM scorecard → L2 segmentation and cadence → L3 mining → L4 predictive performance → L5 collaboration orchestration → L6 named relationship owners → L7 composite.

The thesis

Standalone is never enough. A scorecard becomes influence only when transactional truth, contract obligations, governance cadence and ownership are wired together.
