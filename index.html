Episode 01 · The front door

Intake & Need Identification

Everything in procurement starts here — the moment a need appears and someone has to buy something. Get intake right and the entire cycle runs clean. Get it wrong and you spend the next fourteen steps firefighting maverick spend, rework, and shadow buying.

Below: the plain concept → how every platform does it (fair A vs B vs C) → then we stack process mining, AI, orchestration and people on top until you see why standalone is never enough.

L0 · The Concept

What it is

The path from a raw need ("I need 50 laptops") to a structured, routed, policy-checked request that's ready to become a catalog order or a sourcing event. It is procurement's demand capture layer.

Why it exists

To capture demand at the source, route it to the right process, and enforce policy and risk checks early — before money leaks out through off-contract, maverick, or shadow buying.

What good looks like

One front door · no-wrong-door guided intake · auto-routing by category & risk · policy-as-code checks up front · high self-service adoption · short intake cycle time.

L2 · Best Practice

Design principles
  • One front door — a single "no-wrong-door" intake for every buy, any channel.
  • Policy-as-code — thresholds, preferred suppliers and risk gates checked at request time.
  • Deflect & guide — push routine buys to catalogs; reserve sourcing for what needs it.
  • Demand aggregation — cluster like requests before they fragment into 40 POs.
  • Clear taxonomy — category tags at intake drive everything downstream.
KPIs that matter
  • ⏱️ Intake cycle time — request submitted → routed & approved
  • 🚪 % through the preferred channel (vs. shadow/email/maverick)
  • ✅ First-time-right rate — requests needing no rework
  • 🔁 Reopen / bounce rate — approval ping-pong
  • 😊 Requester satisfaction — adoption is a leading indicator of leakage

Standalone, the P2P suite handles requisitions but bounces approvals serially and can't see its own bottlenecks. Add orchestration and the four reviews run in parallel. Add a GenAI copilot and the requester never sees a form. Add process mining and the whole loop gets continuously tuned from its own event log. That is the synergy — and no single vendor sells all four honestly.

Cheat Sheet — Intake & Need

The 5-second definition

Turn a raw need into a structured, routed, policy-checked request — before money leaks.

Do

One front door · guided routing · policy-as-code · deflect to catalog · aggregate demand.

Don't

Multiple entry points · email/Slack requests · approvals in series · no category tag at source.

Platforms in one line

Zip/ORO lead on no-wrong-door intake; Coupa on UX; Ariba on network depth; Oracle on ERP-native; GEP/Ivalua on configurability.

The layers

L3 mining finds the bounce · L4 AI kills the form · L5 orchestration parallelizes reviews · L6 clarifies who owns what.

The thesis

No single vendor does intake + orchestration + AI + mining well. Value is in the seams.
